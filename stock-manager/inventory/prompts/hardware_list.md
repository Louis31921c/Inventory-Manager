This is a hardware list: a printed form filled in by hand in the workshop. Read the
handwriting.

Header fields, each next to its printed label:
- list_date: the date field, as YYYY-MM-DD (often written day-first, e.g. 24/08/2026 is 2026-08-24).
- site: the job site field, exactly as written (it may include a job number).
- work_item: the structure or part of the building the hardware is for.
- drafter: the name or initials of whoever filled the sheet in.

The table lists one entry per written line, top to bottom:
- designation: the hardware as written by hand, expanded to normal spelling, e.g. "Bolt M6x30",
  "Washers M6", "Nut M6", "Stud M10x100", "Insert M6".
- article: the same item as a short clean name in capitals, following the house rules below, so the
  same product carries the same name here as on a delivery note (e.g. "BOLT M6X30", "WASHER M6",
  "NUT M6", "STUD M10X100").
- reference: the reference column when filled, otherwise null. It is often empty on these sheets.
- quantity: the number in the quantity column.
- stock: the number in the stock column when one is written, otherwise null. A line can carry a
  quantity, a stock figure, both, or neither.
- Ignore blank ruled lines and the empty tick column used later for "in stock / to order".

Handwriting is often ambiguous: 1 and 7, 0 and 6, M6 and M8. When a figure could be read two ways,
put the reading you think is right and add a warning naming the line and the doubt. Use null rather
than a guess for anything you cannot read at all, and add a warning for it.

warnings: short notes for the person checking the sheet: unreadable figures, a second sheet visible
behind this one, lines crossed out or corrected, anything already ticked.
